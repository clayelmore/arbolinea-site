    Concept

    The concept card

    A concept card shows one kind of produce and every code it owns. You open it from the Library, from a scan, or from a card on Home.

    Picture, name and score

    The card starts with the produce picture, its name, its family and its Density Score. When you open the card by scanning a sticker, a line under the picture names the code you scanned, such as You scanned 4011, and that code's row carries a Scanned tag.

    Codes

    The Codes section has one row for each code, shown as a large number. The line Organic = 9 + PLU above the rows states the organic rule once. Chips on each row say what makes that code different:

    • Size, such as S, L or All Sizes
    • Region: East/Central or West
    • Grow method, such as greenhouse or field
    • Form, such as bunch or seedless

    Codes used everywhere come first. Codes for one region follow under a region heading. If you set a region, the card leaves out the other region's codes. Each row also has a star. A starred code is part of your study set.

    Labels on a code row

    Known
    You know this code. A code counts after a correct answer in under 5 seconds with no hint.
    LATER
    The app teaches the most common size or region of an item first. The other codes wait until you know that one.
    Retired or Non-US
    IFPS retired the code, or restricts it from use in the United States and Canada. The code is shown for reference and left out of practice.
    Still in use
    A retired code that you starred back into your training.
    Store Pack or My Store
    A store code from an imported pack, or one you added yourself.

    Mastery and note

    Mastery counts how many of the codes you study are Known, for example 2 of 3 studied codes known. A NOTE card gives a coaching tip when one exists.

    Actions

    Study opens flashcards for this item. Practice starts a short belt drill on it. Compare opens the nutrition explorer with this item on the board. Viewing the card and Compare are free. Study and Practice need the full app unless the item is in Level 1.

    On a screen at least 600 points wide, the card has two columns. A shopper sees the nutrition first and the codes after it.
