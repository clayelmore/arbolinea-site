Which courses do we update first?

Most training teams have more courses than time. This tool helps decide which ones to update first. Rate each course on five signs of need: how many people take it, what goes wrong if they get it wrong, how out of date it is, whether it works for everyone, and what learners say about it. We combine those into a need score from 0 to 100 and rank the list.

Effort is not part of the score. A course that matters a lot and needs a rebuild should still rise to the top, so we show effort as a label that helps you plan the work. The ranking supports your team's decision. It does not make it.

Results

Using the results

  • Required courses sit above the ranking. Only the required change is mandatory, not a full rebuild. If safety or legal content is wrong right now, we suggest pulling it or posting a notice now, before the update.
  • Consider retiring appears when reach and importance are both low. Check with the owner first.
  • Consider merging appears when you mark a course as overlapping another.
  • Close call means a 10-point change in one weight would move the course to another group. Talk those through.
  • Before a rebuild, ask whether a job aid or a process fix would solve the problem. Our format chooser can help.

How the score works

Small numbers point to the sources at the bottom of the page.

Each need factor is rated 1 to 5. A 1 adds nothing and a 5 adds the factor's full weight. We add those up and scale the total to 0 to 100. Every point on every scale has a label, because survey research generally finds fully labeled scales give more reliable answers.1

Why effort is not in the score

In a weighted score, a high mark on one factor can make up for a low mark on another.2 If effort counted against a course, a hard update could hide an urgent need. A 2018 ISPOR Special Task Force report on decision scoring recommends leaving cost out of the score as a negative factor.3 Some product teams divide by effort instead, as RICE does4 and WSJF does,5 but with WSJF, big but important work can wait.6 Nielsen Norman Group's impact and effort guidance pursues quick wins and big bets alike.7 So effort is a label that helps you plan.

Why must-change courses sit outside the score

Portfolio guidance says to name required work as required, not force it to win a scoring contest.8 One audit guide adds that outdated content should not stay available just because most of it is still valid.9

About learner feedback

A 2008 meta-analysis found that learner reactions mostly reflect the characteristics of the course itself.10 For use on the job, ratings of how much people liked a course had a corrected correlation of .08, and ratings of how useful it was had .17, when the two were measured separately.11 So the feedback factor asks about usefulness and clarity, and carries the smallest weight. High completion does not show that a course works either.12

Reviewing the whole catalog

Course audits usually sort courses into keep, update, merge, or retire.13 Low use is a signal, not a verdict: a small audience can be critical, or people may not find the course.14 Accessibility and courses that no longer run belong in the review too.15 Flash content, for example, has been blocked since January 12, 2021.16 Cathy Moore's flowchart asks whether a job aid or a workflow fix would solve a problem before training does.17 We suggest asking the same before a rebuild.

The ranking supports your decision

Decision scoring guidance is clear that the analysis informs people and does not decide for them.18 UK government guidance recommends showing what each factor contributed; we show the top two.19 The close call marker is a simple version of the sensitivity testing the UK manual recommends.

Our choices

These settings are our judgment, not findings from a study. Change the weights and capacity to fit your team.

  • The default weights: reach 25, importance 30, out of date 20, works for everyone 15, learner feedback 10.
  • The starting cutoffs: 70 for Do first and 45 for Next.
  • With a capacity number, Next holds the same number of courses as your capacity.
  • The warning when more than half the list is marked Must change.
  • Counting "No data" for feedback as a 3.
  • The 12-month window for reach.
  • The 10-point test for a close call.
  • The retire hint: reach and importance both 2 or less.
  • The effort labels: Quick fix for 1 or 2, Moderate for 3, Major project for 4 or 5.
  • The middle levels (2 and 4) of Works for everyone.
